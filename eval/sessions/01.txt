User: No, what does KoLeo actually do mathematically?
Assistant: KoLeo is a differential entropy estimator based on k-nearest neighbors (with k=1). On the unit hypersphere, maximizing the Kozachenko-Leonenko entropy corresponds to minimizing the negative log-distance between each embedding and its nearest neighbor in the batch:
L_koleo = - (1 / n) * sum_{i=1}^n ln(min_{j != i} ||z_i - z_j||_2).
This forces the normalized vectors to spread uniformly over the sphere without requiring negative pairs.

```python
def koleo_loss(p):
    # p shape: (B, D)
    p = torch.nn.functional.normalize(p, dim=-1, p=2)
    dots = torch.mm(p, p.t())
    # Numerical issues here?
    dists = torch.sqrt(torch.clamp(2.0 - 2.0 * dots, min=1e-8))
    dists.fill_diagonal_(float('inf'))
    min_dist, _ = torch.min(dists, dim=1)
    return -torch.mean(torch.log(min_dist + 1e-8))
```
Assistant: That formulation avoids explicit `torch.cdist` overhead by leveraging the identity ||x - y||^2 = 2 - 2*(x . y) on unit-norm vectors. Clamp the inner argument to prevent negative numbers under float precision before sqrt.
